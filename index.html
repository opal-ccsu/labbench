Practice benches for Psy 215 and Psy 210 · Dr. Caleb Bragg

Choosing is the skill. Clicking is the follow-through.

Software will run whatever you tell it, including the wrong thing, without complaint. These four benches exist so you can rehearse the choosing: which test, why, what the output should look like, and what sentence to write about it. They run in your browser on data they make up. Nothing you type is sent anywhere, and nothing here is submitted or graded.

I have numbers and no sentence.

The Interpret stage of any lab shows the APA shape and a plain-language sentence after you have written yours. The fastest way to learn the rules themselves is the Concept Bench's Find the error: p is never .000, df has to match the design, and "significant" is not a finding until you say which groups and which direction.

I have to order data from an AI and log it.

The AI Data Protocol tab on either bench runs a pretend AI with the real failure modes: miscounted rows, out-of-range values, data that are too clean, a table cut off halfway. You write the spec, catch what is wrong, and assemble the four-part log. Practice it here before it counts.

The benches

Four stations. Every one of them makes you write first.

Each is one page that works offline once it has loaded. Progress, where a bench keeps any, stays in your own browser.

BENCH 01Excel

Psy 215 Lab Bench

All ten Excel data activities, run live on the handouts' own scenarios. You specify the dataset, the bench draws it with the truth built in, you verify it, then work the sheet with formula cards that show exactly what each cell does. Includes the Which test? chooser, the AI Data Protocol practice loop, and the sample prompts for the Team Mastery assignments that need data.

Best for seeing an activity through before you do your own, and for watching what changes when you draw again.

BENCH 02SPSS

Psy 210 Lab Bench

All thirteen SPSS labs with SPSS-style output and the syntax that produces it. Specify, verify, analyze, interpret, then draw again. Labs 11 and 13 use teaching replicas built to the codebooks of the real files handed out in lab, so you can rehearse the screening before the file arrives.

Best for learning to read an output table before the lab, and for the Comprehensive Practical, where nobody tells you which tests to run.

BENCH 03Both courses

Concept Bench

The vocabulary and the ideas as typed practice. Seven modes: define a term in your own words, name it from its idea, fill the blank, work the number with fresh values every time, name the test for a scenario and say why, find the error in a report, and fill the map from memory. Terms and numbers are checked exactly; explanations are checked for their key ideas, then you compare with the glossary entry and mark yourself.

Best for the week before an exam, and for finding out what you only think you know.

House rules

How to get something out of them.

Write first.

Every bench asks you to type before it shows you anything: the sentence before the model sentence, the test name before the verdict, the definition before the glossary entry. That order is the whole method. If you skip it, you are reading, and reading is not practice.

Guess, then check.

The Estimation Habit works here the way it works in Excel and SPSS. Before the bench draws a sample, say where the mean will land. Before it runs the test, say whether p will be small. Being wrong on the bench costs nothing and teaches the most.

The bench's numbers are never yours.

Everything here runs on data the bench made up. Your lab runs on data you ordered, verified and logged, and on an interpretation you wrote. Use the bench to rehearse, to see what a result should look like, and to check your reasoning after you have done your own.

Practice only. These tools do not replace your work.

Copying a sentence from any bench into your write-up, or using its worked answers in place of your own thinking, is the prohibited use of AI and technology described in your syllabus. It offloads the thinking, and the thinking is the assignment. The benches are built so that their numbers never match yours, and so that every model answer opens only after you have written your own.

The handouts are the assignment. The benches are where you practise them.
